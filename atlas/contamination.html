Mold and pink streaks.

Fuzzy growth on the surface, or streaks of pink, orange or red running through the jar. Almost everything else in this Atlas is fixable. This one is not.

How common
Uncommon
Severity
Terminal — discard
Visible when
In the jar

AT-11
§ 01 — what you're seeing The symptom.

Fuzzy growth in any colour — white, green, black, blue — sitting on the surface or the walls of the jar. Or streaks and patches of pink, orange or red running through the starter itself. Sometimes a smell that is genuinely rotten rather than merely sour.

This is the one entry in the Atlas where the answer is throw it out. Not scrape and continue, not feed it back to health. Discard the whole thing, wash the jar in hot soapy water, and start a new starter.

Before you do — make sure that's actually what you're looking at. Two normal, harmless things get mistaken for this constantly, and far more starters get thrown away by mistake than are ever genuinely contaminated.

§ 02 — root causes Why it happened.

Ranked by likelihood. Start at the top before assuming something exotic.

  1. what it usually ISN’T
    Grey or dark liquid on top
    That is hooch — alcohol from a hungry culture. It can be grey, brown or almost black, and it can smell strongly of solvent. It is not mould, it is not dangerous, and the fix is to feed the starter. Liquid is not growth. If it pours, it is hooch.
  2. what it usually ISN’T
    A dry, darkened crust around the rim
    Starter that has dried on the jar wall oxidises and goes grey-brown. It can look like a colony. Scrape a little off — if it is hard and flaky rather than fuzzy, it is just dried starter. Wipe the rim and carry on.
  3. the real thing
    Mould — fuzzy, raised, three-dimensional
    Mould has texture. It stands up off the surface, it is fuzzy or velvety, and it usually appears as distinct circular colonies rather than an even layer. Colour is unreliable — it can be white, so "white so it is fine" is not a safe rule. Texture is the tell: flat and wet is fine, fuzzy and raised is not.
  4. the real thing
    Pink, orange or red — bacterial contamination
    Streaks or patches of pink through the starter indicate bacterial contamination, most commonly Serratia marcescens. It is not mould and it does not look fuzzy, which is why people talk themselves out of it. Any pink, orange or red that is not from something you deliberately added means discard. This one has no ambiguity.
  5. why it happened
    Too little acidity, usually from underfeeding
    A healthy starter defends itself by being acidic — the pH is low enough that most contaminants cannot establish. Contamination usually follows a long neglect, a very wet or warm environment, or a starter that was never fully established. It is rare precisely because a well-fed culture is a hostile place for anything else.
§ 03 — the fix What to do.
  1. Confirm it is not hooch or dried crust
    Pour off any liquid and scrape the rim first. Then look at what is left. Fuzzy and raised, or pink/orange — discard. Flat, wet, and merely discoloured — feed it and see how it looks tomorrow.
  2. Discard all of it, not the top layer
    Mould sends hyphae well below the visible surface, so scraping off the top does not remove it. There is no safe amount to keep. Bin the contents, and do not compost it indoors.
  3. Wash the jar properly, or replace it
    Hot water and detergent, and a dishwasher cycle if you have one. Scratched plastic containers hold onto contamination in a way glass does not — if it was plastic and scratched, throw the container out too.
  4. Start again, and change what caused it
    Building a new starter takes about two weeks. Before you do, work out what let it happen: an unwashed jar, months of neglect, a warm humid spot, or a culture that never got fully established. Feeding on a schedule is the actual prevention.
  5. Next time, keep a backup
    This is the argument for drying some of your starter. Ten minutes of work and a jar of flakes in a drawer turns this from starting over into a four-day inconvenience.

Before next bake — check these

  • Is it liquid? Then it is hooch, not mould — feed it.
  • Is it fuzzy and raised, in any colour including white? Discard.
  • Any pink, orange or red at all? Discard. No exceptions.
  • Discard everything, not just the visible part.
  • Wash the jar in hot soapy water, or replace it if it is scratched plastic.
  • Dry a backup of the new starter once it is established.
